# Produce distinct options and converge without hiding disagreement

Use this reference for ideation, co-design, critique, or workshop support.

## Define the creative boundary

Start with the problem statement, affected people, evidence, constraints, and decision. Separate fixed constraints from inherited preferences that remain open to challenge.

Write one prompt that describes the desired progress without embedding a feature. Add a second prompt for the main failure or exclusion risk when responsibility matters.

## Diverge by mechanism

Generate responses from different mechanism families:

- remove or prevent the problem;
- change a policy, rule, default, or incentive;
- clarify with content or communication;
- change the human service or operating process;
- add software assistance;
- automate a bounded step;
- transfer control to the affected person;
- keep current behavior while gathering named evidence.

Vary who acts, when they act, what information changes, and how recovery works. Do not count cosmetic variants as distinct options.

Use analogies only to reveal how an idea works. Do not reuse a competitor's surface treatment without checking the different user, incentive, and operating context.

## Make options concrete enough to compare

For each option, state:

- the mechanism and affected behavior;
- the user benefit and burden;
- the assumptions it depends on;
- technical and operational requirements;
- organizational or economic effect;
- trust, privacy, accessibility, misuse, and unequal-impact concerns;
- the condition that would make it fail.

Combine options only after each mechanism is understood. Combining too early hides tradeoffs.

## Converge through declared criteria

Choose criteria before scoring. Weight them only when a real decision owner has a reason to prefer one outcome.

Use evidence to compare options. Mark unknowns instead of assigning invented scores. A matrix organizes judgment; it does not create evidence.

Keep at least one rejected option and its reason in the decision history. This prevents later agents from repeating discarded work without new evidence.

## Facilitate participation fairly

For a group session:

1. share the decision, evidence, and constraints before the meeting;
2. let participants generate ideas independently before discussion;
3. collect ideas without attaching seniority to the first review;
4. invite affected people to contribute where consent and power conditions allow;
5. surface disagreements, risks, and missing voices;
6. converge using declared criteria and a named decision owner;
7. end with a test, owner, and completion signal.

Do not force affected people to disclose personal experience publicly. Co-design participation does not transfer the burden of final delivery or safety to participants.

## Use a short working session only when it helps

A useful 60-minute pattern is:

- 10 minutes: decision, evidence, and constraints;
- 10 minutes: individual mechanism generation;
- 15 minutes: share and cluster by mechanism;
- 15 minutes: compare against criteria and evidence gaps;
- 10 minutes: select the assumption, test, owner, and next decision.

Change the timing to fit the decision. Do not hold a workshop when one person can make a reversible choice from existing evidence.
